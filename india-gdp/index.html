SSUJITH WITH AI

AI experiment · 11 Oct 2026

How rich would India be today if Britain had never ruled it?

I gave five AI models the same detailed research prompt and asked each for a single number: India's 2026 GDP in US dollars, in a world without British colonial rule.

These are AI estimates of an alternate history, not facts. Counterfactual history can't be proven; the value is in seeing how different models reason, and where they agree.

The starting point

24.4%
India's share of world GDP in 1700
Angus Maddison (2003), undivided India, PPP 1990 int. $. About 20% at today's borders.

GPT-6$6.6T

Values in US$ trillions. Middle answer (median): $7.3T, almost double today. Average of all five: $8.4T. Haiku's working had an arithmetic error (its own numbers gave $9.0T, not $9.5T), so treat its figure with caution. GPT-6's answer came from ChatGPT; the other four from Claude models.

What they agreed on

  • India would be richer. Every model put it between 1.6× and 2.8× today's GDP.
  • Nobody kept the 24%. Europe's Industrial Revolution would have shrunk India's share anyway. One model estimated about three-quarters of the fall since 1700 was not caused by colonial rule.
  • The biggest disagreement is the currency. How much a richer India's rupee would be worth in dollars moves the answer more than history does.

My own run

I re-ran one model (Opus's) changing one assumption at a time: unification wars, a Licence-Raj-style economy after 1947, population, price levels, an earlier industrial push. The answers ranged from $5.4T to $9.1T, so my own estimate is about $7–7.5T.

How the experiment was run

  • Each Claude model ran separately, with web access, and could not see the others' answers.
  • Each had to use primary sources (Maddison, the Maddison Project Database 2023, the IMF), show its calculations, build four scenarios and challenge its own conclusion.
  • All were anchored on 1700 (India ≈ 24% of world GDP), converted to modern India's borders.
  • I checked every model's arithmetic and the IMF figures at the source.

The exact prompt

Paste it into any AI model and compare its answer with the ones above. Ask it to show its calculations, then challenge its assumptions.

Show the full prompt
# Independent Economic Research: What Would India's GDP Be in 2026 Without British Colonial Rule?

Act as an independent economic research team consisting of:

* An economic historian specializing in India (1600–2026)
* A macroeconomist specializing in long-term growth
* A development economist specializing in Asian industrialization
* A quantitative economist specializing in counterfactual modeling

## Primary research question

**If Britain had never colonized or politically ruled India, what would India's actual nominal GDP in US dollars be in 2026?**

I want your most defensible independent estimate, not a patriotic narrative, an anti-colonial argument, or a repetition of commonly quoted figures.

Your primary output must be a **single estimated 2026 nominal GDP figure in USD trillions**, with a justified range.

Also calculate PPP GDP separately for economic validation. Do not confuse nominal GDP with PPP GDP.

## 1. Counterfactual assumptions

Assume:

1. The British East India Company never establishes territorial political control in India.
2. The British Crown never governs India.
3. Britain can still trade with Indian states.
4. No other European power automatically replaces Britain as India's colonizer.
5. The European Industrial Revolution and global technological advances still occur.
6. India eventually becomes one unified country with approximately the same territory as the Republic of India in 2026.
7. Other historical developments, including internal wars, political fragmentation, religious conflicts, social institutions, and regional competition, remain possible.
8. Do not automatically assume India becomes a democracy, a technological superpower, or a wealthy industrial nation.

Explain how realistic the eventual unification assumption is and how it influences your estimate.

## 2. Historical economic baseline

Research India before and during British colonial rule.

Use original sources wherever possible, including:

* Angus Maddison's original historical GDP estimates
* Maddison Project Database 2023
* Peer-reviewed economic-history research
* World Bank and IMF data
* Research on the economic history of India, Japan, China, and Thailand

Investigate:

* India's share of world GDP in 1700, 1750, 1820, 1870, 1913, and 1950
* GDP per capita across these periods
* India's percentage of world population
* India's share of world manufacturing
* India's participation in international trade
* India's industrial and technological capabilities before colonization

Pay particular attention to Maddison's estimate that India accounted for approximately 24.4% of world GDP in 1700.

Verify the source and the accounting methodology.

Do not assume India could have retained that world-GDP share indefinitely.

**Important:** Distinguish historical India, including territories now in Pakistan and Bangladesh, from the modern Republic of India. Reconcile boundaries wherever possible.

## 3. Economic impact of British colonialism

Independently evaluate the economic impact of:

* Colonial taxation and wealth transfers
* Deindustrialization and destruction or displacement of traditional industries
* The decline of India's textile exports
* Competition from European mechanized manufacturing
* Restrictions on Indian trade and industrial policy
* Railway, port, and communication infrastructure
* Literacy, education, and human-capital development
* Domestic entrepreneurship, savings, banking, and capital investment
* Agricultural productivity, famine, and land institutions
* Political stability and internal economic integration
* Industrial technology adoption
* Long-term institutional effects after independence

For every factor, distinguish:

**A.** Changes caused by British colonialism

**B.** Changes that might have happened regardless of colonialism

**C.** Possible economic benefits of colonial-era developments

**D.** Uncertainties that cannot reliably be quantified

Do not assume that all extracted wealth would have become additional GDP. Distinguish financial transfers, capital stocks, productivity, and annual economic output.

## 4. Build your own quantitative economic model

Use at least two independent methodologies.

### Method A: Historical growth accounting

Use a suitable economic production function, such as Cobb–Douglas:

Y = A × K^α × (hL)^(1−α)

Estimate the potential effects of alternative developments in:

* Capital accumulation
* Human capital
* Population
* Labor productivity
* Total factor productivity
* Industrialization
* Institutional quality

Model the additional or reduced annual real GDP-per-capita growth that India might have experienced during:

* 1750–1870
* 1870–1947
* 1947–1991
* 1991–2026

Use compounding to calculate the resulting economic difference in 2026.

Show the exact rates, time periods, calculations, and assumptions.

Do not arbitrarily select annual growth rates merely to produce an impressive final GDP number.

Ensure physical capital, human capital, and TFP improvements are not double-counted.

### Method B: International comparative development

Compare India's hypothetical trajectory with relevant countries, including:

* Japan
* China
* Thailand
* Indonesia
* Other appropriate Asian economies

Evaluate how political independence, institutional capacity, education, investment, market integration, and technological modernization affected economic performance.

Explain why the comparisons are useful and where they are misleading.

Do not assume India would automatically follow Japan or China.

Use these comparisons to challenge and constrain the historical growth model.

If the two methodologies disagree materially, explain the disagreement instead of averaging the estimates without justification.

## 5. Construct four independent scenarios

Develop:

1. **Pessimistic:** No British rule, but political instability or weak institutions prevent effective modernization.
2. **Conservative:** Limited improvements, slower industrialization, and modest productivity gains.
3. **Central estimate:** The most defensible historical counterfactual based on the balance of available evidence.
4. **Optimistic:** Successful earlier reforms, industrialization, infrastructure, and sustained productivity development.

For each scenario, calculate:

* Population in 2026
* Real GDP-per-capita growth assumptions
* GDP-per-capita difference compared with actual India
* PPP GDP in 2026
* Nominal GDP in 2026, USD trillions
* Nominal GDP per capita
* Share of world nominal GDP
* Share of world PPP GDP
* Main economic mechanisms
* Major uncertainties

Explain why the central estimate is more defensible than the other scenarios.

Do not choose the central estimate simply as the arithmetic midpoint of the other scenarios.

## 6. Nominal GDP conversion — critical requirement

My main question concerns **nominal GDP in current US dollars**, not purchasing-power-parity GDP.

Calculate real output first, but then determine how to translate hypothetical India's output into a plausible nominal USD figure.

Investigate:

* Actual India's 2026 GDP at PPP and nominal exchange rates
* The relationship between income per capita and domestic price levels
* Whether a wealthier hypothetical India would have a different nominal-to-PPP conversion ratio
* Whether relevant Asian comparator countries can help constrain that ratio
* Exchange-rate uncertainty and the Balassa–Samuelson effect

Do not automatically apply India's actual nominal-to-PPP ratio without evaluating its suitability.

Present at least two plausible nominal conversion assumptions and show the impact on your result.

If a precise nominal GDP prediction is not defensible, provide an explicitly conditional central estimate and a wider nominal valuation range.

Nevertheless, give your best reasoned **single-number nominal GDP estimate** rather than avoiding the question.

## 7. World GDP shares

Use the latest IMF World Economic Outlook data actually available by **October 11, 2026**.

Verify the publication date. Do not use reports released after this date.

Calculate world-GDP shares using:

**Method A:** Hypothetical Indian GDP divided by actual 2026 world GDP.

**Method B:** Hypothetical Indian GDP divided by adjusted world GDP, replacing India's actual output with the hypothetical output.

Explain the difference and use consistent nominal and PPP denominators.

Do not compare India's 1700 historical PPP GDP share directly with its modern nominal GDP share.

## 8. Sensitivity and uncertainty

Test how the central estimate changes with:

* Different annual productivity growth advantages
* Different industrialization starting dates
* Different education and investment improvements
* Different hypothetical populations
* Different nominal exchange-rate assumptions
* Stronger or weaker political institutions

Identify which three assumptions have the greatest influence on your final GDP estimate.

Indicate whether your uncertainty range is a statistical confidence interval or a judgment-based scenario range.

## 9. Challenge your own conclusion

Before finalizing, critically examine your result.

Ask:

* Have I underestimated India's precolonial structural weaknesses?
* Have I overestimated the effects of British extraction?
* Have I assumed that retained wealth would automatically be invested productively?
* Have I underestimated the economic benefits of railways, administrative integration, and international trade?
* Have I sufficiently accounted for European and Asian technological development?
* Have I assumed modern political unification too easily?
* Would the resulting GDP per capita be economically plausible compared with other Asian countries?
* Am I inadvertently selecting assumptions to justify a predetermined GDP figure?

Revise the estimate if this review reveals inconsistencies.

## 10. Required final answer format

Begin with a direct conclusion:

**"Our independently estimated 2026 nominal GDP for India without British colonial rule is $___ trillion, compared with India's actual GDP of $___ trillion."**

Then provide:

1. Executive summary
2. Historical GDP evidence and sources
3. Economic impact of British colonialism
4. Quantitative methodology and equations
5. Four-scenario GDP comparison table
6. Central estimate calculations
7. Nominal GDP and exchange-rate methodology
8. World GDP share calculations
9. Sensitivity analysis
10. Strongest evidence supporting and contradicting the estimate
11. Main limitations and uncertainties
12. Final estimated nominal GDP and reasonable uncertainty range

End with a simple table:

| Metric                                | Result |
| ------------------------------------- | ------ |
| Actual India GDP in 2026, nominal USD |        |
| Pessimistic GDP estimate              |        |
| Conservative GDP estimate             |        |
| **Your central nominal GDP estimate** |        |
| Optimistic GDP estimate               |        |
| Central PPP GDP estimate              |        |
| Central GDP per capita                |        |
| Central world nominal GDP share       |        |
| Central world PPP GDP share           |        |
| Main reason for your estimate         |        |
| Confidence in your central estimate   |        |

## Research integrity requirements

* Work independently. Do not attempt to reproduce estimates from other AI models.
* Do not assume a predetermined GDP figure.
* Cite credible academic research, preferably peer-reviewed, for important historical claims.
* Provide working source links.
* Clearly separate established historical facts from assumptions and speculative counterfactuals.
* Show calculations so the result can be audited.
* Do not invent historical data, citations, precision, or statistical certainty.
* Where data are unavailable, acknowledge the gap.
* If you cannot access live sources, disclose that limitation rather than claiming verification.

**The objective is to determine the most economically plausible 2026 nominal GDP of India in a world where British political colonization never occurred—not to support any preferred historical or political narrative.**

## Starting point (added)

Anchor the model on 1700, with India at about 24% of world GDP (Maddison: 24.4%). State that this is a PPP figure in 1990 international dollars for undivided India (including today's Pakistan and Bangladesh), convert it to Republic of India borders, and don't assume the share persists. Show how the 1700 anchor feeds into 1750, 1820, 1870 and then 2026.
