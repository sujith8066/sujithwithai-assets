The one-page money rule

For any team where one person can send money. Copy the box below into your team chat or handbook, fill in the two blanks, and agree on it together. It costs nothing and takes ten minutes.

Why now

On 5 Nov 2026, US prosecutors in New York charged three men over a scheme that allegedly used AI copies of CEOs' voices and faces on calls to get employees to wire money to a "confidential deal". Fake lawyers then called to make it look real. Prosecutors say it took more than $129 million from companies including two hospital systems, a bank, a fintech company, a mutual fund company and a pet food company ($5,242,747). These are charges, not convictions: the defendants are presumed innocent.
Source: US Attorney's Office, Southern District of New York, press release 26-317 (5 Nov 2026).

The rule (copy this)

OUR MONEY-OUT RULE

1. No money leaves because of a call, video call, email or chat alone.
   Before any new payment, or any change to someone's bank details,
   we call back on a number we ALREADY have on file.
   Never use a number, link or contact from the message itself.

2. Two people approve every payment over $_____.
   One person asks, a different person approves.

3. We have a code word: agreed in person, never written in chat or email.
   Anyone asking for an urgent payment must say it. No code word, no payment.

4. "Keep this confidential" or "don't tell anyone" means STOP and call back.
   So do: urgency, a new bank account, a deal you've never heard of,
   or lawyers and advisers you've never met.

5. Nobody gets in trouble for slowing down a payment to check.
   That includes checking on the CEO.

Agreed by: _____________   Date: _________

Red flags on a money call

  • Secrecy: "this is confidential, don't discuss it with colleagues".
  • Urgency: "it has to go out today, before the deadline".
  • A new account: a bank account you have never paid before, often abroad.
  • New people: lawyers or advisers you have never dealt with, introduced on the call.
  • Odd channel: the boss suddenly uses a new number, app or meeting link.

If money already went out

  • Call your bank straight away and ask them to recall the payment. Speed matters most in the first hours.
  • In the US, report it to the FBI at ic3.gov. Elsewhere, report it to your national police fraud line.
  • Keep everything: call logs, emails, meeting links, account numbers.
